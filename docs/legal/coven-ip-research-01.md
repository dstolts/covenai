# "The Coven" / "Coven" -- IP & Trademark Research
**Date:** 2026-03-24
**Purpose:** Evaluate "The Coven" as a name for jitai.co's open-source community engagement / gamification framework
**Methodology:** Web research across USPTO databases, Trademarkia, GitHub, domain registrars, music industry databases, gaming IP, cultural analysis

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Overall Assessment: CAUTION (borderline BLOCKED for standalone "The Coven")**

The name "The Coven" has significant conflicts in the community/coworking space (The Coven, Minneapolis -- $12.5M valuation, 8 locations, actively expanding via franchise, Wefunder fundraising). "Coven" alone collides with the active rock band Coven (Jinx Dawson, touring 2024-2025), Riot Games' League of Legends Coven skin line, Coven AI (crypto/AI platform), Coven Works (tech talent company), Code Coven (game dev accelerator), and Crypto Coven (NFT project). Using "Coven" as part of a compound name (e.g., "CraftCoven") has significantly fewer conflicts but cultural sensitivity around witchcraft terminology remains a factor for enterprise clients.

## 3. "ONE TIN SOLDIER" SONG RIGHTS

### Copyright Ownership
- Written by: Dennis Lambert and Brian Potter (1969)
- Original copyright: ABC/Dunhill Music, Inc. (1969)
- Current copyright holder: **Songs of Universal, Inc.** (BMI) -- part of Universal Music Group
- First recorded by: The Original Caste (1969)
- Billy Jack soundtrack version: Coven (1971)
- The song has been covered 50+ times by various artists.

### Using the Theme as Inspiration
- The melody and lyrics are fully copyrighted by Universal Music Group. This is NOT public domain.
- **You CANNOT use the melody** in any form without a synchronization or mechanical license from Songs of Universal, Inc.
- **You CAN use the moral theme** (peace over greed, community over conflict) as philosophical inspiration -- themes and ideas cannot be copyrighted.
- An original composition inspired by the anti-war, community-first ethos of "One Tin Soldier" is 100% legal as long as:
  - It has an original melody (no melodic similarity)
  - Original lyrics (no borrowed phrases)
  - No sampling of the original recording
- Quoting or referencing the song in marketing copy (e.g., "inspired by the spirit of One Tin Soldier") is generally acceptable as nominative fair use, but using the song itself requires licensing.

**Verdict: CLEAR for thematic inspiration, BLOCKED for melody/lyrics use without Universal Music Group license.**

## 8. RELIGIOUS/CULTURAL SENSITIVITY

### Witchcraft Association
"Coven" literally means "a group or gathering of witches." This is its primary dictionary definition. Using it commercially carries inherent cultural weight.

### Historical Context
- The Sephora "Starter Witch Kit" controversy (2018) is the landmark case: mass backlash from pagan/Wiccan communities over commodification of sacred practices. Product was pulled.
- Crystal Coven Mystic Cottage (Kentucky): received threats and harassment from a local pastor; community members accused them of "witchcraft and Satanism."
- Broader trend: pagan/Wiccan communities are increasingly vocal about commodification and cultural appropriation of their traditions.

### Enterprise Client Perception Risk
For jitai.co targeting enterprise clients (Fidelity, Nasdaq, BMO -- financial services):
- "The Coven" carries occult connotations that may create discomfort in conservative corporate environments.
- Financial services compliance and brand risk teams may flag it.
- Religious employees may object to participating in a "coven."
- The witchcraft association is the FIRST thing that comes up in any Google search.

### Mitigation Factors
- "Coven" has been adopted by mainstream brands (The Coven coworking, Code Coven) without backlash in progressive/tech circles.
- In dev/tech culture, edgy names are common and often celebrated.
- The word is English-origin, not borrowed from a closed cultural practice (unlike white sage or specific indigenous rituals).
- Modern witchcraft practitioners generally do not claim exclusive right to the word "coven."

### Assessment
- **Tech/startup audience:** CLEAR -- edgy names are a feature, not a bug.
- **Enterprise/financial services audience:** CAUTION -- brand risk committees may hesitate.
- **Religious sensitivity:** LOW-MODERATE -- some individuals will object, but no organized opposition to commercial use of the word exists.

**Verdict: CAUTION** -- Acceptable for developer community, risky for enterprise-facing brand. Consider audience.
